# Historical claims -- results

Corpus: 33692 poems across 14 eras (1600-1624 to 1925-1949). Eras before 1600 were excluded (too few poems, Middle English).

## How each measure was scored

| measure | scored from |
|---|---|
| nature | word-list rate (% nature words) |
| sacred | word-list rate (% religious words) |
| industrial | word-list rate (% industrial words) |
| concreteness | Brysbaert lexicon (mean) |
| valence | NRC-VAD lexicon (mean valence) |
| arousal | NRC-VAD lexicon (mean arousal) |
| dominance | NRC-VAD lexicon (mean dominance) |
| visual | Lancaster norms (mean visual strength) |
| auditory | Lancaster norms (mean auditory strength) |
| joy | NRC EmoLex rate (% joy words) |
| sadness | NRC EmoLex rate (% sadness words) |
| fear | NRC EmoLex rate (% fear words) |

## The Romantic nature turn -- WEAKLY SUPPORTED

> Romantic poets (~1800) wrote about nature far more than the Augustan poets before them.

- Measure **nature**, expected to **rise**.
- 1750-1774: 2.547  (95% CI [1.733, 3.357])
- 1800-1824: 2.980  (95% CI [2.586, 3.292])

## Secularization -- CONFIRMED

> Poetry grew more secular (less religious) across the 1800s.

- Measure **sacred**, expected to **fall**.
- 1800-1824: 1.443  (95% CI [1.201, 1.661])
- 1900-1924: 0.960  (95% CI [0.861, 1.068])

## The turn to concreteness -- CONFIRMED

> The 1700s were abstract; Romanticism and then Imagism made poetry concrete.

- Measure **concreteness**, expected to **rise**.
- 1750-1774: 2.569  (95% CI [2.526, 2.632])
- 1900-1924: 2.666  (95% CI [2.652, 2.681])

## Industrial imagery -- WEAKLY SUPPORTED

> Industrialization put machines and cities into poetry.

- Measure **industrial**, expected to **rise**.
- 1775-1799: 0.147  (95% CI [0.111, 0.182])
- 1875-1899: 0.188  (95% CI [0.169, 0.207])

## A darker modern mood -- WEAKLY SUPPORTED

> Poetry grew darker / less positive in the modern era.

- Measure **valence**, expected to **fall**.
- 1750-1774: 0.118  (95% CI [0.105, 0.135])
- 1900-1924: 0.108  (95% CI [0.103, 0.113])

## More emotional intensity -- CHALLENGED

> Modern poetry runs at a higher emotional pitch (more arousing language) than earlier verse.

- Measure **arousal**, expected to **rise**.
- 1750-1774: -0.048  (95% CI [-0.056, -0.043])
- 1900-1924: -0.077  (95% CI [-0.082, -0.072])

## The visual turn -- WEAKLY SUPPORTED

> Poetry grew more image-saturated and visual, peaking with Imagism around 1910-1920.

- Measure **visual**, expected to **rise**.
- 1750-1774: 2.393  (95% CI [2.354, 2.444])
- 1900-1924: 2.436  (95% CI [2.420, 2.452])

## A sadder modern mood -- CHALLENGED

> Modern poetry leans more on the explicit language of sadness and loss.

- Measure **sadness**, expected to **rise**.
- 1750-1774: 3.054  (95% CI [2.548, 3.416])
- 1900-1924: 2.725  (95% CI [2.624, 2.817])
